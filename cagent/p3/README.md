This package proves exact maximum transient depths for **every card count from 1 through 23** in Lean 4.34.1. It also proves that every staircase is a fixed partition, and proves eventual convergence to the staircase and uniqueness of the cyclic partition for **every triangular card count**, including zero. Exact convergence times are also verified for the six positive triangular card counts 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, and 21. New general C1 lemmas prove that every partition eventually reaches a cycle, cyclic partitions are exactly binary boundaries, and diagonal energy is non-increasing. Cyclic partitions have largest pile at most one plus their pile count. C1 is solved under the task criteria: its complete written proof supplies the numerical counting argument beyond these Lean theorems. C2 is also solved by a complete written upper/lower argument, with only partial Lean coverage. C4 is solved by a complete written uniform proof with an explicit extremal family. C3 is solved by its written general bound and constructive classification of every maximizer at `T_k−1`. C5 is solved by a complete written upper/lower proof, including all exceptional values. C6 is paused.

Each theorem `maximumDepth_n` proves two statements: every decreasing positive partition of `n` enters some periodic orbit by the displayed depth, and an explicit partition first enters a periodic orbit at exactly that depth. “Depth” is the first periodic state, so it does not assume that the orbit is a fixed point. The state transition removes one card from every pile, discards empty piles, creates a pile whose size is the previous number of piles, and sorts the result. `Basic.lean` defines these terms and proves the general certificate logic. `Enumeration.lean` proves that its executable list contains every partition satisfying `IsPartition`, so the finite upper bounds are exhaustive. The `cbv` tactic produces computation proofs checked by Lean's kernel; `#print axioms` records the dependencies. No `sorry`, custom axiom, `native_decide`, or external solver is used.

## Method fit

Lean has paid off for definitions, the complete cycle classification, arbitrary-size triangular convergence, and exact finite depth certificates: 41 files replay with explicit axiom checks. It is also useful for any new reusable lemma needed by several cells.

For C1’s counting step, a short double-counting argument is more efficient than adding group actions, finite cardinalities, and a list-to-quotient bridge solely to translate a classical formula. [The complete written C1 proof](c1/written-proof.md) supplies that argument. [Independent exact replay](evidence/cycle-counts.json) checks the formula against actual cycles for n=1–40, covering 215,307 partitions in 0.463 seconds. Run `python3 cagent/p3/c1/check_cycles.py` from the repository root. This replay is finite evidence, not a proof for all n; the general argument is readable mathematics and its counting step is not Lean-certified.

For C2, the general upper/lower arguments are complete in writing; formalizing the pile-lifetime lemmas remains optional further assurance. C4 now also has a complete written upper/lower proof. C3 also has a complete written bound and all-maximizer construction. For C5, the explicit extremal family now has a general written lower-bound proof. The audited upper-bound proof closes both cyclic entry families by inverse-tree arguments and a width-two lifetime lemma. An independent direct-cycle replay covers 158,034 partitions through rank 10. The general argument remains written rather than fully formalized in Lean. C6 remains paused.

## Replay

From the repository root, run:

```sh
python3 cagent/p3/verify.py
```

The script uses the package's pinned [`lean-toolchain`](lean/lean-toolchain) and [`lakefile.toml`](lean/lakefile.toml), builds the imports, checks each theorem file with warnings treated as errors, checks its printed axioms against Lean's standard `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`, and writes source hashes, commands, outputs, and timings to [`evidence/verification.json`](evidence/verification.json). It needs an installed Lean toolchain through `lake`/`elan`, Python 3, and no third-party Python package. [`evidence/exploration.json`](evidence/exploration.json) is discovery data only; the Lean proofs do not trust it.

The new classification, triangular-convergence, and cycle-correspondence modules also passed standalone `leanchecker` replay; see [kernel-checks.json](evidence/kernel-checks.json). To repeat from `cagent/p3/lean`, run `lake env leanchecker ProofPursuit.P3.Classification`, `lake env leanchecker ProofPursuit.P3.TriangularGeneral`, and `lake env leanchecker ProofPursuit.P3.Necklace` after the verification script builds their imports.

The Lean sources are under [`lean/ProofPursuit/P3/`](lean/ProofPursuit/P3/): `Basic.lean` for the transition and abstract lemmas, `Enumeration.lean` for partition completeness and sound certificate checks, `Staircase.lean` for all-`k` staircase properties, `Convergence.lean` for finite triangular convergence times, [`TriangularGeneral.lean`](lean/ProofPursuit/P3/TriangularGeneral.lean) for general triangular convergence and uniqueness, [`Boundary.lean`](lean/ProofPursuit/P3/Boundary.lean) for arbitrary-size binary-boundary cycles, [`Eventual.lean`](lean/ProofPursuit/P3/Eventual.lean) for eventual cycling, [`Energy.lean`](lean/ProofPursuit/P3/Energy.lean) for energy monotonicity and the cyclic largest-pile bound, [`Classification.lean`](lean/ProofPursuit/P3/Classification.lean) and its diagonal/reconstruction helpers for the converse, [`RankedClassification.lean`](lean/ProofPursuit/P3/RankedClassification.lean) for the exact word length and weight at every rank, [`Necklace.lean`](lean/ProofPursuit/P3/Necklace.lean) for the cycle/rotation correspondence, `NegativeCheck.lean` for a wrong-depth rejection, and `N1.lean` through `N23.lean` for exact finite maxima.
